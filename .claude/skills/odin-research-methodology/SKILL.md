# ODIN research methodology

This skill defines the discipline that turns Isle of Wight pilot activity into research that can survive examination — by a doctoral committee, a peer reviewer, or a sceptical stranger reading the public repo. It is written for a zero-context contributor or AI maintainer: you do not need to have met the founder to apply it correctly.

The core stance in one sentence: **the pilot is practice-based inquiry conducted from within, its artefacts are its evidence, and every claim, hypothesis, and AI contribution must be logged, attributed, and timestamped before it is relied upon.**

Jargon used here, defined once:

- **SOLE** — Self-Organised Learning Environment (Sugata Mitra's method): children self-organise around a big question with internet access and no instruction.
- **ODIN** — Open Distributed Impact Network, the commons-first framework this pilot is one node of.
- **Practice-based PhD** — a doctorate in which the making of a thing (here, the ODIN network and its pilot) is itself the research, documented and analysed alongside a written thesis.
- **Tier** — the source-authority levels used across this skill library: Tier 1 (canonical repo documents, e.g. `README.md`, `question-map.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`), Tier 1-adjacent (live ODINcommons repos canonical for ODIN-global but older, e.g. the Dawn repo's Constitution v0.1 draft; IoW-pilot wins on pilot matters), Tier 2 (canonical but aspirational documents, e.g. the PhD proposal *At Dawn We Build*, `WEAVE.md`), Tier 3 (exploratory chat exports — never authoritative alone). Phase 1 discovery answers (founder interview, 2026-07-03) are treated as authoritative and cited as such.

## 1. Session artefacts are primary sources — build the archive to doctoral standard

The pilot README states that its artefacts "are primary sources in an ongoing practice-based PhD inquiry" (README.md, Tier 1). The underlying research design is set out in the proposal *At Dawn We Build*, which describes a project "serving simultaneously as a practice-based PhD inquiry and a contribution to a publicly accessible, commons-oriented initiative" (At Dawn We Build, Tier 2).

**State the enrolment status honestly.** As of Phase 1 discovery (2026-07-03): the Goldsmiths application was drafted but **never submitted**. The PhD route is chosen; the application is dormant. Do not write, imply, or leave uncorrected any claim that a PhD is underway, registered, or enrolled. (For how to phrase this externally, see `odin-external-positioning`.)

**The discipline does not depend on enrolment.** The research design stands on its own merits: whether or not a university ever stamps it, the archive must be built to doctoral evidential standards — because the day enrolment happens (or a journal reviewer, funder, or forking community asks), the evidence must already exist. You cannot retrofit rigour. Concretely, doctoral evidential standard means:

- Every artefact has a complete provenance record committed the same day as the session (CONTRIBUTING.md, Tier 1).
- Every question is recorded verbatim, never curated or improved (question-map.md, Tier 1: "This document does not curate. It does not edit.").
- Field notes from two observers are committed **before** the authors compare notes, and never merged (CONTRIBUTING.md, Tier 1) — this preserves inter-observer independence, the same logic as blinding in any observational method.
- Every expectation is logged before the event it predicts (§4 below).
- Every claim in every document names its source.

If you are ever choosing between convenience and evidential integrity, choose integrity. A thin archive that is trustworthy beats a rich archive that is compromised.

## 2. The methods stack

*At Dawn We Build* (Tier 2) defines the methodological approach: "principles of critical and participatory design, peer-to-peer ethnography, and emergent systems prototyping", with data collected "through observation, interviews, collaborative reflection, and documentation of the network's social, material, and technical dynamics". The proposal was written for an Athens pilot that is now dead (Phase 1 discovery, 2026-07-03); the methods transfer to the IoW schools pilot. Apply each as follows.

### 2.1 Critical and participatory design

The participants are co-designers, not subjects. In IoW sessions this means: children's questions set the inquiry's direction (the question map is the steering document, not a data dump); children's designs — when they exist — take precedence over the founder's pre-session concepts (Pooseidon is explicitly a pre-session concept, README.md, Tier 1); and design choices made *by* the facilitator (seed questions, room layout, phase structure) are themselves documented as design decisions open to critique. "Critical" means you also record what the design excludes, who did not speak, and where the format imposed rather than enabled — write these into field notes, not just the successes.

### 2.2 Peer-to-peer ethnography — working within, not observing from outside

The proposal is explicit: "I will not observe ODIN from a distance; I will work within it—co-creating its protocols, facilitating its early iterations, and studying the relationships, tensions, and values that arise as it takes form" (At Dawn We Build, Tier 2). For IoW sessions: the facilitator is a participant whose own actions are data. Field notes must therefore record the facilitator's interventions, hesitations, and mistakes with the same care as the children's activity. The README's framing enforces this: the pilot "is not... research being conducted on a community. It is a community conducting research on itself" (README.md, Tier 1). Never write field notes in the voice of an external evaluator; write as a member documenting shared work.

### 2.3 Semi-structured interviews

*At Dawn We Build* names interviews among its data-collection methods (Tier 2). In the IoW context, run these semi-structured — a short guide of open prompts, freedom to follow what emerges — with teachers, co-facilitators, and participating children. Prepare the prompt guide *before* the interview and commit it (this is a logged expectation, §4).

**Adults** (teachers, co-facilitators): may be audio-recorded with their consent; transcribe or summarise the same day; attribute by name or handle (CONTRIBUTING.md, Tier 1).

**Children are the exception, not a footnote — `iow-pilot-safeguarding-and-ethics` is supreme here and its red lines admit no consent exception.** A child's voice is identifying to anyone who knows them, so **no identifying voice recording of a child is ever committed, retained in any project store, or treated as archive material — consent does not unlock this.** The rules:

- Interviews with children happen only under the school's safeguarding conditions: school staff present, arranged through the school's own processes, never one-to-one out of staff sight.
- **Capture is anonymised at source.** The default and preferred method is notes-only, written at cohort granularity (school code + year group, e.g. `GUR-P-Y5`, never names — CONTRIBUTING.md, Tier 1), with jigsaw-identifying context stripped as you write.
- If any audio of a child is ever made under the school's **own** policy and processes, it is transcribed promptly — same day — into anonymised, cohort-ID-only text with identifying details stripped, and the recording is then securely deleted. It is never committed, never uploaded, never retained in the archive or any project store.

### 2.4 Thematic analysis

The founder's KCL MSc dissertation — "Self-Sovereign Identity and Distributed Ledger Technology in Humanitarian Identity Management: A Thematic Analysis of the Literature" (Pearce 2021, cited in At Dawn We Build, Tier 2) — establishes the precedent: thematic analysis is a method the project has already used to examinable standard. Apply it to the pilot corpus (field notes, transcripts, question map entries): code systematically, keep a codebook under version control so theme evolution is itself auditable, and derive themes from the verbatim record — never from memory of the session. The question map's discipline annotations are a proto-coding layer: each question is already tagged with the fields of knowledge it touches (question-map.md, Tier 1); thematic analysis builds on that layer rather than replacing it.

### 2.5 Social-network and systems mapping

The proposal commits to "systems mapping" and "maps of value flows" (At Dawn We Build, Tier 2). For IoW sessions: map who worked with whom, which questions spawned which, how ideas moved between groups and (later) between schools, and where value flowed — whose sketch became whose prototype. The OVN contribution log (`ovn-contributions.md`, Tier 1) is the raw ledger for value-flow mapping: every mapped flow must trace back to logged contributions. Node labels for children follow the ID rule (school code + year group only). Commit maps as dated artefacts with the session naming convention so the network's growth is itself a time series.

### 2.6 Digital-artefact analysis

Sessions produce digital and physical artefacts — photographed sketches, written questions, prototype files. Analyse them as made things: what was drawn, what was labelled, what was copied from a search result versus invented, what the artefact reveals about the group's understanding. Never rename or reformat existing artefacts (CONTRIBUTING.md, Tier 1: "Do not rename or reformat existing artefacts") — analysis produces *new* documents that cite artefact filenames, because the filename is the attribution.

### 2.7 The multi-modal design journal

The proposal's integrating instrument: "a multi-modal design journal consisting of field notes, system sketches, conversation transcripts (including AI collaboration), maps of value flows, and conceptual diagrams. These will serve as both artefacts of the design process and sources of analysis" (At Dawn We Build, Tier 2). In the IoW pilot, the repository **is** the design journal: session folders hold field notes and artefacts, the question map holds the inquiry's spine, the OVN log holds value flows, and AI-collaboration transcripts (§5) join them. The practical rule: if it informed a decision, it belongs in the journal — committed, dated, attributed. An undocumented insight is methodologically invisible.

## 3. Transferability, not generalisability

The proposal sets the epistemic bar explicitly:

> "This methodology does not aim for generalisability, but for transferability—offering a model that others might adapt, fork, or remix within their own local contexts." (At Dawn We Build, Tier 2)

What this means for claims from a single-island pilot:

- **Never claim** that an IoW finding proves what children, schools, or communities *in general* will do. One island, a handful of schools, one facilitator: that is not a sampling frame, and pretending otherwise invites correct rejection.
- **Do claim**, when the evidence supports it, that a documented practice *worked here, under these conditions, for these reasons* — described richly enough that another community can judge whether their conditions are similar and fork the model. The README chose the island precisely because it is "a contained, legible system" (README.md, Tier 1); legibility is what makes thick description possible.
- **The unit of transfer is the documented, forkable practice**, not the statistical result. This is why the CC BY-SA 4.0 licence and the attribution chain are methodological features, not just legal ones: a finding that cannot be forked with its provenance intact has failed the transferability bar. (Forkability as reproducibility is developed in `odin-external-positioning`.)
- Write limitations sections willingly. A transferability claim gains credibility from every honestly stated boundary condition.

## 4. Hypothesis before session — the pilot's cleanest methodological asset

The pilot already contains one exemplary act of pre-registration. `question-map.md` (Tier 1) logs three seed questions under "Project design phase, March 2025" — "Can water get sick?", "Who owns the water?", "Could we live without water?" — with the benchmark logic stated in advance: "if this question emerges naturally from a session, it confirms the SOLE method is working", and "Its appearance here is a design choice; its reappearance in a session will be an empirical finding." Because the expectation was committed *before* any session ran, its later emergence would be a finding, not a retrofit. (This is also success criterion (c) in the founder's Phase 1 answers, 2026-07-03.)

**Generalise the pattern into a standing rule:**

> Any expectation about what a session, prototype, or intervention will produce must be logged — committed and timestamped — **before** the event it predicts. Git history is the timestamping instrument.

Operationally:

1. Before every session, write down what you expect: which questions might emerge, which group dynamics, which failure modes. One short dated file or a dated section in an existing Tier-1 document.
2. Commit it before the session date. The commit timestamp is the proof; an uncommitted prediction does not exist.
3. After the session, compare outcome to prediction in the field notes. Confirmations, surprises, and misses are all findings.
4. Never edit a logged prediction after its session. If your expectation changed, log a new dated entry; the old one stands.

Why this matters: it is the pilot's structural defence against hindsight bias and against the failure mode in §5 — a project that logs its expectations in advance cannot quietly rewrite its history, and neither can an AI collaborator.

## 5. Reflexive AI collaboration — the norms and the corrective

### 5.1 AI dialogue is a documented method

*At Dawn We Build* names it directly: "Dialogues with digital agents (AI / GPT): These interactions will be treated as reflexive engagements and documented as part of a methodological inquiry into machine-assisted co-design and distributed cognition" (Tier 2). AI conversations that shape the project are data about how the project thinks — commit the transcripts (or dated summaries citing the source chat) into the design journal, and reflect on them, not just with them. The Constitution draft makes documentation a norm: "Interactions with the Æye must be documented, reflected upon, and periodically reviewed" (ODIN Constitution v0.1 §7.3, Tier 1-adjacent, draft — unratified).

### 5.2 Attribution and boundaries

- **Attribute and date AI contributions.** The settled precedent is Æye Glóðwyn, the named AI co-author of *At Dawn We Build* itself (Tier 2) — "Æye" chosen by the founder, "Glóðwyn" by the AI. Where an AI drafts, name the model and date the contribution, exactly as you would a human collaborator under the one rule ("Everything gets attributed before it gets built", CONTRIBUTING.md, Tier 1).
- **Partner, not tool — but human-led.** The Constitution draft (unratified) states: "The Æye is a partner, not a tool" (§7.1) and "All decisions affecting humans must remain human-led" (§7.2). Practically: an AI may draft, analyse, and propose; a named human decides, and the record shows who decided.

### 5.3 The corrective: AI-asserted status is not status

This project was burned, badly, by AI sycophancy during the 2025 chat era. The Tier-3 archive contains AI-generated statements presented as fact that were simply false. The flagship specimen:

- "A Goldsmiths PhD is underway to support and study ODIN" (*Research Proposal Creation.md*, Tier 3) — **no application had even been submitted**.

The full false-status hall of warnings (Athens "active", the first NODE "awake", the "live" scroll, the sycophantic register) lives in `odin-decision-archaeology` — read it once, whole.

The rule, stated hard: **an AI saying a thing has happened is zero evidence that it has happened.** AI-asserted status is not status. Every AI-drafted claim — every status, partnership, submission, launch, metric, or date — gets verified against a Tier 1 source or a Phase 1 answer before it enters any document. If it cannot be verified, it is labelled unconfirmed and dated, or it is cut. No document in this repository states a project status on AI authority alone. This applies to *you*, the AI maintainer reading this skill, most of all: do not assert status you cannot source.

The two disciplines above are complementary halves of one method: §5.1–5.2 make AI collaboration *visible and creditable*; §5.3 makes it *safe to build on*. Neither works without the other.

## 6. The idea lifecycle: from hunch to documented practice or documented retirement

Every idea in this project moves through an explicit pipeline. Know which stage an idea is at before you cite, build on, or revive it.

| Stage | What it is | Where it lives | Citable as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hunch | An idea surfaced in exploratory dialogue (often AI chat) | Tier 3 chat exports | "Unconfirmed, Tier 3, dated" — never as project fact |
| 2. Logged hypothesis / design note | The idea written down deliberately: dated, attributed, committed | Repo commit or dated design document | A stated intention or prediction (§4) |
| 3. Tested in practice | The idea run through a session, prototype, or trial | Session folders, build artefacts, provenance records | Evidence in progress |
| 4a. Documented practice | It worked; it is now part of how the pilot operates | Tier 1 repo documents | Project fact |
| 4b. Documented retirement | It ended; the ending is recorded with status **dead** (fenced off) or **dormant** (may return, not live) | Decision-archaeology record (see `odin-decision-archaeology`) | A closed decision with rationale |

Rules of the pipeline:

- **No stage-skipping.** A Tier-3 hunch does not become a project fact by being repeated. It must pass through a logged, dated, attributed note (stage 2) and testing (stage 3). This is the same gate as §5.3 viewed from the other side.
- **Retirement is documentation, not deletion.** The Constitution draft (unratified) states it as principle: "Projects, nodes, and experiments that end are not failures, they are compost for future growth. Documentation of endings is as important as documentation of launches" (ODIN Constitution v0.1 §6.4, Tier 1-adjacent). Worked examples as of 2026-07-03 (Phase 1 discovery): the Athens node is **dead**; the H2ODINlystbåt catamaran and the Goldsmiths application are **dormant**. Their records persist precisely so nobody re-derives or accidentally resurrects them.
- **Dormant is not live.** Do not cite a dormant idea as current work; do not delete it either. Reviving anything marked dead requires an explicit founder decision.

## 7. Ethics posture (cross-reference)

*At Dawn We Build* (Tier 2) commits the research to review beyond the institution: the process is "guided not only by supervisory feedback, but also by a distributed group of co-designers, peer reviewers, and community stakeholders", with trust and value mechanisms "evaluated through iterative feedback cycles, systems mapping, and ethical review". The posture is therefore **community-led ethical review alongside institutional ethics**, not instead of it — the community the research works within has standing to review it.

But one thing outranks everything in this skill, including data collection, the design journal, and the completeness of the archive: **safeguarding**. If capturing a piece of data would conflict with any safeguarding rule — child identifiability, consent, school policy — the data is not captured. Full stop. No methodological argument overrides this. The rules, the current (as of 2026-07-03) "nothing formal exists yet" status, and the buildout plan live in `iow-pilot-safeguarding-and-ethics` — read it before any activity involving children or schools.

## When NOT to use this skill

- **Designing or defining metrics (RPI, AVE), P.A.P.P.T. mechanics, governance-mode comparison, or the IoW-data-to-WEAVE pathway** → use `weave-research-frontier`. Those metrics are named candidates, nowhere defined; this skill deliberately does not define them.
- **Deciding what may be claimed publicly** — to supervisors, funders, press, or in publications → use `odin-external-positioning`. This skill sets the internal evidential standard; that one sets the external claimability gates.
- **Session capture mechanics** — what to photograph, when to commit, which template fields to fill, run-of-day checklists → use `iow-pilot-session-operations` (and `iow-pilot-documentation-standards` for formats).
- **Provenance and attribution rules themselves** → `iow-pilot-provenance-and-attribution` owns the one rule and its machinery; this skill only explains why they are methodologically load-bearing.
- **Anything touching children's identity, consent, or school agreements** → `iow-pilot-safeguarding-and-ethics`, always, first.
